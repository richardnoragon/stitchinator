Crochet terminology and starter stitches

Standard stitch names and abbreviations change; prose stays in US English. Custom labels and edited instructions remain yours. Review instructions after applying. Includes base stitches, picots, turning chains, tall stitches, clusters, popcorns, front/back post relief stitches and loop variants. Turning-chain presets state whether they count; do not also add the same chains through radial construction rules. Picot is a decorative zero-count attachment. Cluster, shell and increase/decrease definitions specify their exact stitch counts; they are examples, not universal recipes.

Assembly preview

Arrange piece copies with controlled folds and seam guides. Colors follow yarn bands. This concept view does not sew edges, simulate drape, or change yarn estimates. Units are centimeters; rotations are degrees.

Controlled fold for selected copy

Up to eight parallel hinges per copy, applied in list order. Crease positions refer to the original flat piece. At 0° the piece is flat; at ±180° the chosen side folds back. Angle sign controls depth direction. Folds do not change fabric dimensions or yarn usage. Reset placement also removes the fold; Undo recovers it.

Seam relationships

Edges are named on the original flat piece, before rotation or folding. Rectangle edges run clockwise from top-left; triangle edges run left-base → tip → right-base → left-base. Circle/oval edges are clockwise quarter arcs, with the top arc starting at the upper-left diagonal. Solid colored guides mark matched edges; dashed lines connect matching start, midpoint and end. These guides do not move or sew pieces.

Alignment rotates and translates the second copy to match edge direction and endpoint midpoint. It never stretches fabric; curved, folded or unequal edges may retain a mismatch. Review other seams after applying.

Shaping suggestions and settings

For round 2 onward, suggest evenly distributed 1→2 increases or 2→1 decreases using the previous round’s count. This is a count-based sample plan, not a guarantee of flatness or finished dimensions. Manual chart editing remains available.

Stitch placement and shaping counts

Sequential placement assigns each symbol its declared number of consecutive base stitches, starting at 1. Round 1 works into the starting ring. Numbering follows your working order after any turn, not positions in the diagram. Choose the physical starting stitch, loops and attachment points yourself. Shared bases, skipped stitches and chain spaces require custom notes.

Positive slot changes identify increases; negative changes identify decreases according to your stitch definitions. Counted starting chains add one to the round total separately. These counts do not predict whether fabric lies flat or reaches the desired dimensions.
